# The Cleaning Queen

A local business worth pitching. This file is everything known about them, and
it is the only thing the build session is allowed to put on the page.

Every line below says where it came from. `[verified]` means it was read off
their own site or listing, with the address it was read from. `[inferred]` means
it was worked out and nobody has checked it. Anything not written here does not
go on the page — a page that states the wrong opening hours to a real business
loses the deal, and a made-up review is worse than no page at all.

Source for everything here: research recorded in the vault at
`02 Business/Leads and Pipeline.md`, done by web search 2026-08-31. Nothing has
been re-checked since, and nothing below came from speaking to them.

## What they have now

A Facebook page with **805 likes, 10 reviews, and 100% recommend**.
`[verified — Facebook, 2026-08-31]`

**No website at all.** In place of one they use a `cutt.ly` link shortener
pointing at an intake form. `[verified]` That is a stronger version of "no site"
than the other leads on the pipeline — there is not even a landing page.

## What would make the page worth their money

They already want online booking. Nobody improvises a link shortener to a form
unless enquiries matter to them and the current route is in the way. `[inferred]`
So the page's job is to **be** that intake form, done properly, with a real
business around it — not to be a brochure with a form buried at the bottom.

Named competitors in the same market who do have real sites, with reviews:
Hill Country Cleaning (hillcountrycleaning.co), Juany Cleaning Service
(juanycleaningservice.com), Austin's Maid Service
(austinsmaidservice.com/maid-service-kyle-tx). `[verified — web search]`
When somebody searches for a cleaner in Kyle, those three come up and The
Cleaning Queen does not.

Their reviews are the strongest asset they have and they are trapped on
Facebook, where they do not help anybody who is searching Google. `[inferred]`

## Not known

- **Phone number.** Not found by search. Must come off their Google listing or
  a Facebook message. **Nothing on the page may print a phone number.**
- Email address.
- Street address, and whether they even publish one — many cleaners do not.
- Opening hours.
- Prices, or whether they price by hour, by job, or by square foot.
- What they actually offer: recurring vs one-off, deep cleans, move-outs,
  commercial work. None of it may be listed as a service until they say so.
- Whether they are insured or bonded — never claim this for anyone.
- Service area beyond "Kyle and Buda".
